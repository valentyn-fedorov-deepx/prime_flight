# What a station needs for real time: the uplink and the chunk

Generated by `scripts/station_uplink.py`. Do not edit by hand.

## In short

- One camera sends **4.0 Mbit/s** (1920x1080, 8 fps, H.264, constant bitrate: every one of the 21 full-turn videos of the test set). A station has two cameras: **8 Mbit/s, 60 MB per minute, 3.6 GB per hour**.
- The uplink of the station has to be **above 8 Mbit/s**, or the station falls behind and never catches up.
- A chunk has to be filmed before it can be sent, so **the chunk length sets the delay** and the link only decides how much is added on top: delay of the oldest frame = chunk length x (1 + 8 / uplink in Mbit/s).
- With one-minute chunks a result is one to two minutes behind the camera whatever the link. For results within about ten seconds the chunk has to be 60 frames or less.
- **20 Mbit/s of sustained upload per station** is the sensible target: the upload takes 40 % of the chunk time, which leaves room for retries. Above 50 Mbit/s nothing changes.

## A chunk of one minute (what the cambox writes today)

One minute from both cameras is 60 MB.

| uplink of the station | to upload that minute | a result is behind the camera by | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 Mbit/s | 120 s | grows without limit | half of the video: every minute of recording adds a minute of delay |
| 8 Mbit/s | 60 s | 89–120 s | exactly the video: it holds, with no room for a single retry |
| 10 Mbit/s | 48 s | 77–108 s |  |
| 15 Mbit/s | 32 s | 61–92 s |  |
| 20 Mbit/s | 24 s | 53–84 s | the target |
| 30 Mbit/s | 16 s | 45–76 s |  |
| 50 Mbit/s | 10 s | 38–70 s |  |
| 100 Mbit/s | 5 s | 34–65 s | the floor: the minute itself |

From 10 to 100 Mbit/s the worst delay only moves from 108 s to 65 s: the minute of filming is the floor.

| uplink | 30 frames (3.75 s) | 60 frames (7.5 s) | 120 frames (15 s) | 480 frames (1 min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 Mbit/s | falls behind | falls behind | falls behind | falls behind |

Chunk size per camera: 30 frames = 1.9 MB, 60 frames = 3.8 MB, 120 frames = 7.5 MB, 480 frames = 30.0 MB. A chunk cannot be shorter than one GOP (30 frames) without re-encoding.

## What else the station needs

- **Sustained upload, measured on site.** The number that matters is what the link really carries for hours, not the name of the tariff. A SIM link has to be measured at the gate before it is counted on.
- **Storage for an outage**: 3.6 GB per hour of both cameras. The real-time worker skips the hole after a reconnect and does not catch up; the recording still goes to post-processing.
- **Traffic**: 86 GB per station per day if the cameras never stop. It is the same video that is uploaded today, sent as it is filmed instead of after the event.
- **No new hardware for results in minutes**: the cambox only records and sends, as now. A check that has to answer in seconds on the camera itself (hair policy, safety vests) needs an accelerator in the box; that has not been measured and is the hardware item of the roadmap.
- **An address to send to**: outbound TCP to the ingest of the worker, later with a certificate per cambox (`ARCHITECTURE.md` of the realtime-pipeline repository).

## How the numbers were obtained

The bitrate is read from the files (`ffprobe`); chunk sizes from the chunker (576 chunks of one event: 1.6–2.1 MB, median 1.83). The delay is the arithmetic of the module docstring, with the frame path of 60 ms measured for ten modules on a T4-class card with TensorRT heads.

The arithmetic against the branch run live over a throttled link (one camera, 30-frame chunks, 300 s of the busiest stretch):

| link | measured, median | model, middle of a chunk | measured, 95th percentile | model, first frame of a chunk | measured, worst | keeps up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 Mbit/s | 6.2 s | 6.4 s | 7.5 s | 7.5 s | 8.4 s | yes |
| 4.5 Mbit/s | 5.7 s | 6.0 s | 7.1 s | 7.1 s | 7.8 s | yes |
| 5 Mbit/s | 5.4 s | 5.6 s | 6.7 s | 6.8 s | 7.5 s | yes |
| 6 Mbit/s | 4.9 s | 5.1 s | 6.2 s | 6.3 s | 7.0 s | yes |
| 8 Mbit/s | 4.3 s | 4.5 s | 5.6 s | 5.7 s | 6.3 s | yes |
| 12 Mbit/s | 3.7 s | 3.9 s | 5.0 s | 5.0 s | 5.6 s | yes |

The worst frames belong to the largest chunks (up to 12 % above the median size). At 4 Mbit/s the single camera is exactly at its own bitrate: it holds (the delay does not grow), with nothing to spare.
